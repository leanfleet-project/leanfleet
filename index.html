Overview

Look ahead, then learn to do it fast.

A rollout planner that weighs assign now against not now serves red orders with fewer AGVs, but it is too slow for large layouts. LeanFleet imitates it with a graph neural network (behaviour cloning, then DAgger) that decides fast enough to run online.

Problem

Planned at the start, red at any time.

Layouts

Synthetic factory-like layouts on a grid of one-metre cells; one-lane roads in both directions; random road blocks.

Fleet

Every AGV starts at a warehouse, carries trays and keeps an order until it delivers it.

Orders

Most are planned at the start of the shift; the rest arrive unannounced. Red, yellow and green, each with a feasible deadline.

Question

With the demand fixed, how small can the fleet be while red orders stay on time?

Method

A slow teacher, a fast student.

  1. 1

    Teacher

    Rollout planner

    For the most urgent yellow or green order, try AGVs and not now; simulate each  s ahead on sampled futures; keep the cheapest. Red orders follow a reservation rule.

  2. 2

    Labels

    Expert decisions

    The teacher dispatches training shifts; every decision becomes a label.

  3. 3

    Imitation

    Behaviour cloning

    The graph policy learns to score the teacher's choice above the others.

  4. 4

    Correction

    DAgger

    The student drives; the teacher labels the states it reaches; train again. rounds.

  1. 1

    Every  s

    Read the state

    Road graph with occupancy, AGVs with their plans, waiting orders.

  2. 2

    Policy

    Score

    One score per (order, AGV) pair for the nearest AGVs, and one for not now.

  3. 3

    Decode

    Assign or wait

    Wait if not now takes at least half of the probability. Orders past their deadline go to their cheapest insertion.

  4. 4

    Route

    Execute

    Central time-reservation routing moves the AGVs; the 3D replay drives the same plan with physics.

Live session

Run the factory yourself.

The paper's simulator runs in your browser (Python via Pyodide) with the student network, and MuJoCo replays its plan in 3D. The layout appears in 3D and 2D when you reach this section; pick a fleet size and a dispatcher, press Start, and add your own orders while the shift runs. Click ⇄ to swap the two views.

Within the problem as posed: orders stay with their AGV, the fleet is fixed for the shift, and your order gets the tightest deadline the model allows. Development seeds only (paper seeds are never used here). The browser runs the same Python code as the paper; the student uses the trained weights in numpy and picks the same actions as the trained network on every state we checked, though long runs can drift after near-ties. The 3D view replays the 2D plan open-loop with MuJoCo physics (WebAssembly) under the rules of the paper's replay:  m per cell,  m safety zones, an AGV enters a cell only after its previous occupant has left. It never changes a decision; waiting orders and road blocks are drawn, not simulated. The counters are measured in your browser.

Limitations

What this does not show.

  • Loading times, priorities, deadlines and demand are our assumptions; the layouts are synthetic.
  • The model moves one cell per second and turns instantly; with  m cells that is faster than a MiR100.
  • Fewer AGVs mean longer deliveries; the delivery-time part of our hypothesis is not met.
  • Decision times are charged by a calibrated cost model.
